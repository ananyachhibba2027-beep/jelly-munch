Quick games to play between classes

By Ananya · 17 Nov 2026

You know the gap. One class ends, the next one starts in ten minutes, and the walk across campus takes three. That leaves about seven minutes of standing around, waiting for the professor to plug in the projector.

Seven minutes is too short to start an assignment and too long to just stare at the wall. It's the perfect size for a quick game, as long as you pick the right one.

What makes a good between-classes game

  • It loads in seconds. Campus Wi-Fi is not your friend. Anything that needs a big download is out.
  • Rounds are short. You want something you can finish, not something you have to abandon halfway.
  • It works one-handed. The other hand is holding your coffee, your bag, or the door.
  • It's fine with the sound off. Because the class before yours is still wrapping up.
  • It's free. Student budget. Enough said.

Good picks for the gap

🟩 A daily word puzzle

Word games like Wordle give you one puzzle a day. That's ideal here. You play, you're done, and there's nothing pulling you into "just one more". Bonus: it's something to talk about with the person next to you.

🔢 2048

Slide tiles, merge numbers, try to reach 2048. It's quiet, it's one-handed, and you can stop mid-game and come back after class. Just be honest with yourself about the "one more move" thing.

🦖 The Chrome dinosaur

On a laptop in a lecture hall, type chrome://dino into the address bar and you've got an endless runner with zero loading time. Close the lid when the professor walks in.

🍓 Jelly Munch

Okay, this one's mine. Jelly Munch is classic Snake turned into a squishy jelly. You eat fruit, grow longer and try not to crash into yourself. It runs in your browser, so there's nothing to install, and a level takes a couple of minutes, which fits nicely between lectures.

A few things that make it good for short breaks:

  • The daily challenge. A new one every day, with 3 free tries. Three tries is a natural stopping point, so you don't lose track of time.
  • Short levels. Three candy worlds to work through, and you can play one level and leave it there.
  • Power-ups like ghost, slow-mo and magnet that keep each round a little different.
  • A live leaderboard, which is dangerous if your whole batch starts playing. Ask me how I know.

Sign in with Google and your progress follows you from phone to laptop, so you can pick up the same run in the library later.

How to make sure you actually make it to class

The whole point of a between-classes game is that it ends before class does. A few things that help:

  • Decide the number of rounds first. "Two rounds, then I walk." Deciding up front is much easier than deciding mid-game.
  • Pick games with a built-in stop. Daily puzzles and limited tries do the hard work for you.
  • Play on the way, not at your seat. Okay, not while walking. But play in the corridor, then put the phone away when you sit down.
  • Skip anything with long streak timers or energy bars. Those are designed to keep you coming back, and they will.
💡 Make it social. Challenge a classmate to beat your score before the next lecture ends. It's more fun than playing alone, and you both have to stop at the same time.

Why a quick game is a good break

A short game gives your brain a proper reset between two very different subjects. Going straight from finance to marketing with no gap is a lot. A couple of minutes of something light, with a clear finish, helps you walk into the next class a bit fresher. Much better than twenty minutes of scrolling that you didn't plan on.
